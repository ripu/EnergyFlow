1. What it is and what it is for

EnergyFlow reads the home solar inverter directly, over the local network, and shows on a screen what is happening to the energy right now: how much the panels are producing, how much the house is using, what goes in and out of the battery, and what is being exchanged with the grid.

It answers one question at a glance — am I running on my own sun right now, or am I buying power? — without opening the manufacturer's portal, without waiting for a cloud round trip, and without the household data ever leaving the house.

It answers the same question forwards and backwards too: a forecast says how sun, consumption and battery will go through the end of tomorrow, and the money value says what a day, a month or a year of energy is worth in cash.

2. How to open it

On the wall panel

There is nothing to do. The 32" portrait monitor in the hallway is driven by the Raspberry Pi: when the Pi boots, a full-screen browser opens the dashboard on its own. It has no keyboard and no mouse, it is not meant to be touched or refreshed by hand — it is a sign that updates itself every five seconds. If the screen is black or blank, go to “When something breaks”.

On a phone or a Mac

The same page opens on any device enrolled in the home tailnet, at:

https://<rpi-host>.<tailnet>.ts.net/

It works both at home and away, as long as the Tailscale client on the phone or Mac is running and connected. There is no password to type: the page authorises itself on the first load.

Why there is no plain LAN address. The server listens on the Raspberry Pi itself, not on the home network. Exposing it on the Wi-Fi would make the household telemetry readable by anyone connected — guests, televisions, smart bulbs, anyone who knows the Wi-Fi password. Tailscale gets the same result without opening anything to anyone, so it is used from the sofa too.

On the Mac, without opening a browser

There are also three ways to keep an eye on the numbers without a tab open:

  • EnergyBar — a menu-bar item showing grid, home, solar and battery.
  • System widget — in Notification Centre or on the Desktop.
  • Übersicht — a fixed bar drawn on the desktop background.

All three read address and token from two files in the Mac's configuration directory:

~/.config/energyflow/url      https://<rpi-host>.<tailnet>.ts.net
~/.config/energyflow/token    the server's token

With those two files the widgets work from any network, as long as Tailscale is on — exactly like the page in the browser. How to write them: “Service, logs and token”.

If the url file is missing, the widgets fall back to 127.0.0.1:8003, that is, they look for the server on the Mac: in that case a tunnel to the Raspberry Pi is needed, opened once and left running.

ssh -N -L 8003:127.0.0.1:8003 <rpi-host>

3. How to read the dashboard

The top bar

Time and date, local weather, sunrise and sunset, and on the right the freshness chip: it is the one element that separates “data from right now” from “numbers frozen on a screen”. When it is green and reads in diretta (live), what you see happened less than fifteen seconds ago.

Solare adesso — solar now

The power the panels are producing at this instant, in W or kW. Underneath, in small type, the split between the two strings (stringhe 2,38 + 1,80 kW): if one of them sits at zero in full sun, there is something to look at on the roof.

Casa adesso — home now

How much the house is drawing at this instant. The line below answers the question people actually ask — am I paying for this? — with “tutta dall'impianto” (all from the system), “coperta al 60% dall'impianto” (60% covered by the system) or “tutta dalla rete” (all from the grid).

The flow diagram

Five nodes — panels, inverter, battery, home, grid — joined by arcs. What matters is the direction: the dots travel along the arc the way the energy is going. A node at rest (below 20 W) stays where it is but dims, so the eye stops looking for it.

  • Battery → “in carica” (charging), “in scarica” (discharging) or “a riposo” (idle).
  • Grid → “preleva dalla rete” (importing), “immette in rete” (exporting) or “in equilibrio” (balanced).
  • Inverter → the power passing through the unit, plus its operating mode.

The numbers on the nodes are always unsigned: direction is carried by the phrase and the moving dots, not by a minus sign in front of a figure.

Battery

  • Carica (charge) — the percentage. The notch on the bar is the configured minimum: the inverter will not go below it.
  • Autonomia stimata (estimated runtime) — how long the battery holds at the current draw. It only means something while discharging; while charging it says “in carica”. The alba (sunrise) marker on the bar answers the real question: will it last until morning? If the fill goes past the marker, yes.
  • Salute (SOH) — how much capacity is left compared to when it was new. It drops very slowly; a sudden fall would be news.
  • Temperature — of the battery and, on a large screen, of the inverter.

Andamento di oggi — today's trend

Two curves, solar and home, with the battery charge strip below them on the same time axis. The curve comes from the Raspberry Pi, not from the browser: it survives a reboot of the Pi and a page reload, so the panel comes back with the day already drawn instead of starting from scratch.

Every point is one minute, and it is the average of the twelve samples read in that minute — not the last value, which would be a matter of luck. If the server does not answer, the browser acts as a safety net with the curve it accumulated itself: a partial curve beats an empty card.

The chart no longer stops at “now”: it carries on, dashed, through the end of tomorrow. That part is a forecast, and it is meant to be recognisable at a glance — see its own chapter.

Storico — history

Below the trend sits the Storico section, which looks back over four periods: Giorno (day), Settimana (week), Mese (month), Anno (year). They are picked with the tabs on the left; the two buttons on the right (“◂ prec.” and “succ. ▸”) step back and forth within the chosen period, and the forward one switches off once it reaches today rather than staying clickable and leading nowhere.

It opens on the week, not on the day: today is already drawn two centimetres above, whereas the long archive — the reason this section exists — would stay invisible until somebody clicked.

PeriodWhat it showsHow far back it goes
Giorno That day's per-minute curve, with the battery charge below it, as in “Andamento di oggi” The last 90 days: beyond that, only the day's summary survives
Settimana
Mese
Anno		 One column per day — per month, on the year view: produced and consumed, with grid import/export and battery flows underneath 230 days, imported from the manufacturer's portal and merged with the ones recorded at home

The battery charge strip appears only in the Day view, and only where per-minute data actually exists. It is not decoration: without the state of charge, looking at a past day you cannot tell why the house drew from the grid at a given hour instead of from the sun. In week, month and year it is absent because there is no such thing as “the charge of a period”.

A day can also be opened directly, and the link can be bookmarked or sent to someone:

https://<rpi-host>.<tailnet>.ts.net/?day=2026-08-08

From the keyboard: p cycles the period, [ and ] step back and forth.

A day with no data is not drawn as a flat line at zero — that would be indistinguishable from a house using nothing. It says instead that there is nothing, and why: “either the system was off, or the day is older than the retention window”.

What is exact and what is reconstructed. In the daily roll-up, only PV production comes from the inverter's own counter and is exact. Import, export, charge and discharge are reconstructed by integrating the per-minute averages, because at midnight the device's daily counters reset and that number can no longer be asked for. They are good for comparing one day with another, not for disputing an electricity bill.

Energia di oggi — today's energy

Two bars, and they are two real totals, not a sum of unlike things.

  • Prodotta (produced) — the energy that left the inverter since midnight, split into “autoconsumata” (self-used) and “esportata” (exported). The note underneath gives the panels' DC yield.
  • Consumata (consumed) — what came into the house, split into “dall'impianto” (from the system) and “dalla rete” (from the grid). The note reports battery charge and discharge separately: those are internal flows, not a third slice to stack beside the other two.

Indici di oggi — today's indices

  • Autosufficienza (self-sufficiency) — how much of today's consumption did not come from the grid.
  • Autoconsumo del prodotto (self-consumption) — how much of what you produced stayed in the house instead of being sold.

DC and AC: two different numbers for the same sun

This is where everybody gets confused, and reasonably so: they are two measurements of the same energy taken at two different points of the system.

  • Panel yield (DC) — what the panels produced in direct current, before the inverter.
  • Produced (AC) — what came out of the inverter in alternating current, ready for the house or the grid.

AC is always a little lower than DC, because conversion has its losses. They are never added together: that would count the same sunshine twice.

The sign convention

On screen the numbers are unsigned and carry a phrase, so this rule mainly matters to anyone looking at the raw data (the register panel, the widgets, the API):

ValuePositiveNegative
Gridimport — you are buyingexport — you are selling
Batterycharging — it is storingdischarging — it is powering the house

4. The forecast: what is going to happen

The trend chart does not stop at now: it runs through the end of tomorrow, and a panel below the curves says the same things in words. It does not answer “how much did I produce”, but the question people actually ask in the evening: will the battery get me to the morning, or will I be buying power at six?

How to tell it apart at a glance

This is the important part of the chapter, because measurement and forecast share one chart and must never be mistaken for each other. The forecast part differs in three ways at once:

  • it is dashed, while measured data is a solid line;
  • it is lighter, and it has no filled area beneath the curve;
  • it starts after a vertical line labelled “adesso” (now) — nothing to the right of that line has happened yet. A fainter second line marks midnight, where “tomorrow” begins.

The legend at the top of the card gains a previsione entry with the same dashes, and it only appears when a forecast is actually drawn.

What the panel says

The sentences are ordered by importance, and only the ones the computation can genuinely support are shown:

  • When the battery runs out — the time of day, not “in 9 hours”: a time can be compared with the alarm clock, an interval has to be subtracted in your head.
  • When it is full again.
  • From what time the grid starts being drawn on, which is when you start paying.
  • How much sun is left before midnight, and when there is none it says so instead of showing a zero.
  • How much sun is expected tomorrow, next to the expected consumption: on its own, “43.9 kWh” does not tell you whether tomorrow is a surplus day or an import day.

Top right you get when it was computed and how far it reaches. It is recomputed every quarter of an hour: if that time is hours old, the forecast is stuck and should be treated with suspicion (or it has vanished altogether — see the troubleshooting section).

How much to trust it

The forecast marks its own homework. Every morning it stores what it predicted, and the next day it compares that with what actually happened: the result is the line “Ieri l'avevo sbagliata del …%” (yesterday I was off by …%), and it shows up even when the figure is embarrassing. Until that comparison exists, its place reads “Precisione non ancora misurata” (accuracy not measured yet): blank space would suggest it had been checked and was fine.

There is no “stated accuracy” for this forecast, and none should be looked for. The last line of the panel says what it is built on — for instance “sole tarato su 6 giorni (R² della taratura 0,95)”, sun calibrated over 6 days, R² of the fit 0.95 — and that number is not a percentage of accuracy: it measures how well the relation between irradiance and production explains the days that have already happened, not how close the forecast will land tomorrow. Only yesterday's score says that. Anyone writing “95% accurate” is misreading that line.

Where the numbers come from

PieceHow it is obtained
The sun Forecast irradiance from the weather service, multiplied by a coefficient learned from this system by comparing real sunny days with real production. The nameplate rating would know nothing about orientation, shading and dirty panels; the plant's own data does.
Consumption The median profile of comparable days, quarter-hour by quarter-hour, with weekdays and weekends kept apart — in a home they are two different days. The shape of the median is kept and its level rescaled on recent totals, because consumption drifts with the seasons.
The battery It is not predicted: it is simulated. Starting from the current charge, sun − consumption is integrated quarter-hour by quarter-hour, accounting for capacity, the reserve floor and round-trip efficiency. The times in the sentences come out of that simulation, not out of a guess.

When the forecast warns you not to trust it

An amber band can appear above the sentences, starting with “Previsione poco affidabile:” (forecast not very reliable) and giving the reason. The cases seen in practice:

  • weather unreachable, or hours old;
  • too little history — with two days of data the sun calibration is a line through four points;
  • unstable sun-to-production relation, meaning the calibration did not hold;
  • the Raspberry Pi's time zone differs from the weather service's. This is the worst failure of all precisely because nothing breaks: the forecast keeps working, shifted by an hour, with the solar peak placed when there is no sun.

With no weather the forecast does not claim you will produce nothing — that would be the biggest lie available: it falls back on the median of what this system produced at the same hour over the past seven days, and declares it in the band.

On the wall panel it is there, cut down to one line. What remains is the dashed curve and the first sentence — the battery one, the question a forecast is on a wall for — plus yesterday's score. Technical detail has no audience at three metres, and half a truncated sentence would be worse than none.

The forecast appears on today only. Open a past day in the history section and there are no dashes: next to the measurement of some 15 March, a forecast would only be a way of confusing the two.

5. What it is worth, in money

Below the energy bars — and in the history section, below whichever period is on screen — sits the Valore dell'energia (energy value) card: the same kilowatt-hours already shown above, multiplied by the price of electricity.

It has to be configured first: no tariff, no amounts

Nobody can guess the prices. They go into config.json, on the Raspberry Pi:

"tariff": {
  "currency": "EUR",
  "import_eur_kwh": 0.00,
  "export_eur_kwh": 0.00
}

import_eur_kwh is what you pay for a kilowatt-hour taken from the grid; export_eur_kwh is what you get for one fed into it. After editing: sudo systemctl restart energyflow, then reload the page.

Use the full price from your bill, not the energy component alone. What matters is what one extra kilowatt-hour really costs: energy plus system charges, transport and taxes — that is, the bill total divided by the kilowatt-hours consumed. With the energy component alone the savings figure comes out well below reality, and savings is the number the whole system exists for.

If the tariff block is absent, the card does not appear at all — no zeros, no dashes, no national average price used “for now”. A plausible false amount is worse than a missing one, because nobody goes and checks it, whereas a missing card is noticed immediately. If only the import price is configured, savings and cost appear and the total is omitted: it would be a sum with a term missing.

The three figures, and why the big one comes first

RowWhat it is
Risparmiato
(saved)		 The energy the house took from the system instead of from the grid, priced at what you would have paid for it. It appears on no bill — it is money that never left — and it is almost always the largest of the three. That is why it sits on top, in large type.
Incassato
(earned)		 The kilowatt-hours fed into the grid, at the export price. Usually far lower than the purchase price: which is exactly why it pays to consume while the sun is up rather than to sell.
Speso
(spent)		 The kilowatt-hours drawn from the grid, at the purchase price. It is the only one of the three you can also read elsewhere, and so it comes last.
Beneficio netto
(net benefit)		 saved + earned − spent, written out below the rule exactly like that.

Every row carries the kilowatt-hours it comes from underneath, and the bottom of the card reads “Calcolato a … €/kWh in prelievo e … €/kWh in immissione” — the prices actually used. They are the only thing that makes the four figures above checkable. If one day the amounts look wrong, that line is the first place to look: usually the tariff in the configuration has gone stale.

On the wall panel there are no amounts, and that is a deliberate sacrifice: in that band of the screen the space is already fully spent, and one extra line would be cut in half. A truncated number is worse than a missing one. Between forecast and money, the wall gets the forecast — it says something that is nowhere else on that screen, whereas the money is the kilowatt-hours above multiplied by a price. The money value stays whole on computers and phones.

6. Telling when something is wrong

The dashboard never pretends. If the numbers cannot be trusted it says so, and it says so differently depending on what is broken: these are different faults and they send you looking in different places.

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
in diretta All good: the last reading is less than 15 seconds old. Nothing.
aggiornati 20 s fa A passing delay, between 15 and 60 seconds. An amber banner appears too. Wait. If it goes green again within a minute, nothing happened.
fermi da 4 min The Raspberry Pi is answering, but the numbers are frozen: it is the inverter that stopped responding, not the server. The values stay on screen, dimmed. See “The numbers are frozen”.
nessun dato + BACKEND OFFLINE The Raspberry Pi has not answered for over a minute. The numbers disappear: leaving them would be worse, they would keep looking true. See “BACKEND OFFLINE”.
A red banner with technical text The last error reported by the reader, for example timeout Modbus sul registro 80. That is the clue to carry into the logs. It usually accompanies one of the two cases above.
A struck-through number The value arrived, but the system judges it untrustworthy: outside the physically possible range, or a counter that ran backwards. Look at it, do not trust it. See “A number is struck through”.

Why two states and not one. A live server handing out a reading that is four minutes old is not “offline”: it answers perfectly well, it is the inverter reader that has stalled. Calling both the same thing would send you hunting for the fault in the wrong place.

They are for the computer view and for maintenance (a keyboard plugged into the Raspberry Pi). The wall panel has no keyboard. Press ? at any time to see them on screen.

On automatic the theme follows local sunrise and sunset: light by day, dark by night. A choice made with t is remembered on that device until you cycle back to automatic.

The 3D view opens with 3 or with the “3D” button among the controls, and closes the same way (or with Esc). It is the only heavy part of the project, which is why it loads only when you open it: the wall panel, where nobody looks at it, does not pay for a graphics library it will never use. When you leave, the drawing really stops — and it stops when the window goes into the background too. The numbers it shows are the same ones as the 2D view, not a separate calculation. It is not on the wall panel: there is no mouse or keyboard there to orbit the scene with, and a graphics context left running around the clock would cost something and return nothing.

8. Service, logs and token

Starting and restarting

On the Raspberry Pi, EnergyFlow runs as a system service and comes back by itself on every boot and after every crash.

sudo systemctl status energyflow      # how it is doing
sudo systemctl restart energyflow     # restart it
journalctl -u energyflow -f           # watch what it says, live

By hand, for a test (from the project folder):

python3 invert.py --serve              # port 8003, loopback only
python3 invert.py --serve --debug      # + a register dump on every read

There is exactly one port: 8003. If it is taken, the server refuses to start and says so, rather than quietly moving elsewhere: a panel pointing at the wrong port just stays blank with no explanation.

The wall panel

# restart the graphical session (the data service stays up)
sudo systemctl restart lightdm

# rotate the monitor without rebooting
XDG_RUNTIME_DIR=/run/user/1000 WAYLAND_DISPLAY=wayland-0 \
  wlr-randr --output HDMI-A-1 --transform 270

Where the logs are

One file per day, in the project's log/ folder, next to the SODE:

log/2026-08-09.txt

It holds the readings, the timings in milliseconds, the Modbus reader's errors and also the browser's own messages, which the page relays back to the server: that way you can work out what happened on the wall panel without standing in front of it.

History on disk

Next to the daily logs, in log/energy/, sit the files that hold up the Storico section:

log/energy/2026-08-09.csv    per-minute detail, kept for 90 days
log/energy/daily.csv         one row per day, never deleted
log/forecast/2026-08-09.json that morning's forecast, kept for the score
  • One write per minute, not one per reading: twelve times fewer writes to the microSD card, which is the part of a Raspberry Pi that wears out first.
  • How much space, measured rather than guessed: 42 kB for a full day, 3.69 MB at the retention ceiling.
  • The day is rolled up at 00:05. If the Pi was off at that hour, the day is consolidated as soon as it comes back rather than being lost.
  • Every deletion made by the retention policy is written to the day's log: files never vanish quietly.
  • Days older than the 90 stay in the archive as summaries: those are the 230 days visible in the Week, Month and Year views, imported once from the manufacturer's portal and kept up to date at home ever since.

To switch history off entirely, in config.json:

"history": { "enabled": false }

With history off the Storico section does not appear at all — see the troubleshooting section.

Health

curl -s http://localhost:8003/health

It answers 200 with "status": "ok" when the reader is running and the values are within the range of the possible; 503 with "status": "degraded" and the list of reasons when not. It is a real probe, not a “the process is alive” ping.

The token

In the browser there is nothing to do: the page receives the token by itself on first load. It is needed by the Mac widgets and by any request you make by hand.

# on the Raspberry Pi: read it (generated on first start if missing)
python3 invert.py --print-token

# on the Mac: two files, the address and the token
mkdir -p ~/.config/energyflow
echo "https://<rpi-host>.<tailnet>.ts.net" > ~/.config/energyflow/url
ssh <rpi-host> "cd EnergyFlow && python3 invert.py --print-token" > ~/.config/energyflow/token
chmod 600 ~/.config/energyflow/token

The token lives in the project's .env file on the Raspberry Pi with 600 permissions, and is not in the repository. If it is regenerated, the widgets must be reconfigured.

The url file is why the widgets work without a tunnel: without it they look for the server on the Mac itself (127.0.0.1:8003), which is the old behaviour. The environment variables ENERGYFLOW_URL and ENERGYFLOW_TOKEN work in place of the two files.

Checking that the numbers are the right ones

python3 invert.py --validate

It checks twelve physical invariants against real data — for example that today's produced energy is consistent with instantaneous power integrated over time, or that total counters never run backwards. This is the check that tells you whether the register map is still reading the right things.

# record a few minutes of data and re-check it at leisure
python3 invert.py --capture day.json --duration 300 --interval 5
python3 invert.py --validate --from day.json

9. When something breaks

The wall panel is blank, black, or showing a browser error

In order, from the most likely:

  1. The data service is down → sudo systemctl status energyflow. If it is stopped, sudo systemctl restart energyflow and read journalctl -u energyflow -n 50.
  2. The service is up but the browser never started → sudo systemctl restart lightdm restarts the graphical session; the data service is untouched.
  3. The browser is stuck on a keyring password prompt. This happens when the --password-store=basic option is missing from the autostart line: without it, Chromium asks “Choose password for new keyring” and the page never opens.
  4. The screen is asleep or the cable has been knocked loose. Mundane, and it happens.
The numbers are frozen — “fermi da N min”

The Raspberry Pi is answering: what is not answering is the inverter. The usual causes:

  • The inverter changed address. This is the most frequent cause, and the system defends itself: after five failed reads (about 40 seconds) it searches the whole home network for the inverter, identifies it by the signature of its registers so as not to grab some other device, and carries on. Usually there is nothing to do and the numbers come back on their own.
    The permanent fix, however, is elsewhere: reserve the inverter's address on the router (a DHCP reservation), so it stops changing. Until that is done, the problem returns at every lease renewal.
  • The inverter is off or restarting. Some models go quiet at night: a few isolated timeouts are not a fault.
  • Unstable Wi-Fi or powerline between the inverter and the router. The logs show repeated timeouts, always on the same registers.

What to look at: journalctl -u energyflow -n 100, and the lines containing Poll error or Discovery in today's file under log/.

BACKEND OFFLINE

The server has stopped answering whoever is looking at the page. From the Raspberry Pi:

sudo systemctl restart energyflow
curl -s http://localhost:8003/health

If the service will not come back, the error message in the logs almost always says why. The two recurring cases are port 8003 already taken by another process (the server writes it to the log and stops on purpose) and an SD card that is full or has gone read-only.

If the wall panel works but the phone does not, the problem is not the server but the link: check that Tailscale is connected on the phone.

A number is struck through

The system received that value but considers it untrustworthy: it falls outside the physically possible range (a four-digit battery voltage, say) or it is a counter that ran backwards.

This is not a dashboard fault, it is the dashboard doing its job: it would rather flag a doubt than show a clean, false number.

curl -s http://localhost:8003/health     # lists suspect fields and failing invariants
python3 invert.py --validate             # full check against real data

If a field stays suspect for long, the register map needs revisiting for that field — which is exactly the signal this mechanism exists to raise.

The weather says “non disponibile” (unavailable)

The weather is fetched by the Raspberry Pi, not by the browser, and cached for a quarter of an hour. When it is missing:

  • the coordinates are absent from config.json (the location section);
  • the Raspberry Pi has no internet connection at that moment.

It is not serious: without weather the rest of the dashboard keeps working, but the automatic day/night theme and the sunrise marker lose their reference.

The Mac widgets are not updating

In order:

  1. The address is missing. Without ~/.config/energyflow/url the widgets look for the server on the Mac itself and do not find it. Write it (see above), or reopen the tunnel ssh -N -L 8003:127.0.0.1:8003 <rpi-host>.
  2. Tailscale is off on the Mac: with a tailnet address the widgets depend on it exactly as the browser does.
  3. The token is missing or stale. The file ~/.config/energyflow/token must hold the current token; if the server generated a new one, rewrite it (see above). Without it the server answers 401 and the widget stays empty rather than faking it.
  4. Check the round trip by hand: 
    curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $(cat ~/.config/energyflow/token)" \
     "$(cat ~/.config/energyflow/url)/data" | head -c 200
The forecast is missing, or stuck hours behind

The “Previsione” card only appears when there is something true to say. It disappears, on purpose, in these cases:

  • You are looking at a past day in the history section. That is intended: on a day already gone, a forecast means nothing.
  • Not enough points are left in the future: the forecast has expired — the recomputation should run every quarter of an hour and is not running. Check the service (journalctl -u energyflow -n 100) and look for the forecast lines.
  • It has never been computed: that happens on first start, and until there is enough history to calibrate the sun. In the meantime the amber band says so.

If it is there but the amber band warns you, the reason is written in it: weather unreachable, too little history, unstable calibration, or the Pi's time zone differing from the weather service's. The last one has to be fixed on the system (timedatectl), not in the application: with the wrong clock the forecast keeps working, shifted, which is the worst way to be wrong.

curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $(python3 invert.py --print-token)" \
     http://localhost:8003/api/forecast | head -c 400
python3 invert.py --forecast-backtest 5     # replay the forecast over the last 5 days
No amounts in euro anywhere

That is the intended behaviour when the tariff block is missing from config.json: with no prices the dashboard shows no amounts rather than inventing them. Add it and restart the service — see “What it is worth, in money”.

If the amounts are there but look wrong, read the line at the bottom of the card (“Calcolato a … €/kWh”): it states the prices actually used, and it is almost always a tariff left stale in the configuration.

On the wall panel no amounts ever appear, tariff or no tariff: that is not a fault, it is the choice explained at the end of that chapter.

The history chart is empty (or the section is missing entirely)

The panel always says which case it is, and the wording changes accordingly:

  • “Nessun dato per questo giorno” — that day the system was off, or the day is older than the retention window (90 days of per-minute detail).
  • “Giornata appena iniziata” — no minute consolidated yet. Normal in the first minute after a start-up.
  • “Storico non raggiungibile” — the backend did not answer at all: the same problem as BACKEND OFFLINE, seen through a different window.
  • “Storico non disponibile” with a code — the backend answered, but said no. The code is in the message and in the day's log.

If the Storico section is missing entirely, history is switched off in config.json ("history": {"enabled": false}): the endpoint answers 404 and the page hides the section rather than showing two buttons that lead nowhere. Turn it back on, restart the service, and the section reappears by itself.

If the files exist but stop growing, look for the history write lines in log/: a full or read-only microSD card is the usual cause.

10. Frequently asked questions

Does the data leave the house?

No. The only thing the Raspberry Pi asks for from outside is the weather forecast, and it asks for it itself using the local coordinates: no request leaves the browser towards the internet.

Why is “Solare adesso” at zero when the sun is out?

Look at the freshness chip first: if it says “fermi da…”, the number is not up to date, it is not zero. If it says “in diretta” and the value really is zero, either the inverter is restarting or production is below the measurement threshold — which happens at dawn and dusk.

Why is “Prodotta” lower than the panel yield?

Because they are two different measuring points, and converting from direct to alternating current has its losses. See DC and AC.

If I reboot the Raspberry Pi, do I lose the day's curve?

No, not any more: the curve lives on disk on the Pi, not in the browser. After a reboot the page comes back with the day already drawn, and past days stay browsable in the Storico section.

How long is the data kept?

Per-minute detail for 90 days; the summary of each day forever, because it takes a handful of bytes. Keeping every minute forever would mean constant writes to the microSD card for minutes from two years ago that nobody will ever look at. The archive currently holds 230 days: everything predating the home panel came from a one-off import out of the manufacturer's portal.

How accurate is the forecast?

There is no single answer, and anyone giving one is guessing. The page states how wrong it was yesterday — that is a measured number — and until it has a comparison it says so (“Precisione non ancora misurata”). The value in brackets next to “sole tarato su N giorni” is not an accuracy figure: see “How much to trust it”.

The forecast shows zero production — is something broken?

During the day, almost certainly not: with no weather the system does not forecast zero, it falls back to the median of the past seven days and declares it in an amber band. If it really shows zero in broad daylight, see “The forecast is missing”.

Why can't I see any money figures?

Either the tariff block is missing from the configuration (no prices, no amounts: the dashboard does not invent them), or you are looking at the wall panel, where amounts are deliberately absent. See “What it is worth, in money”.

Can I open the dashboard from my phone at home without Tailscale?

No. The server listens on itself only: from the home network — Wi-Fi included — the page does not answer. That is intended (see “How to open it”), and it holds from the sofa too.

Can the panel be left on all night?

Yes, that is what it is for: it switches to the dark theme by itself at night, and the animations are light.

How fresh are the numbers?

The Raspberry Pi polls the inverter every five seconds and the page re-reads at the same rate. Past fifteen seconds the chip turns amber; past a minute the numbers disappear.

Are there values the system does not show?

Yes, on purpose. Of the register map, only the verified fields are published; the ones not identified with certainty are left out rather than guessed. A plausible but wrong number is worse than a missing one.

Who can see the dashboard?

Whoever is physically in front of the panel, and whoever is enrolled in the home tailnet. There is no port open to the internet.
